Keeping vertical rhythm when the design system changes

A baseline grid is only useful if the shipped page still sits on it. Here is how we check ours.

Every type size in our system has a line height that is a multiple of 24 points. Headings get 48, body copy gets 24, captions share a line with their neighbours. On paper this gives the page a steady rhythm that readers feel without noticing.

Where rhythm breaks

The trouble starts in the browser. A margin of 1.5em on a 17 point paragraph is 25.5 points, not 24. A button that is 42 points tall pushes everything below it off the grid by a little, and a little adds up over a long article.

So before a release we turn on the same grid the designers used, right over the running page, and scroll. Anything that drifts off a line is a bug with a number attached to it.

The same 6-column, 960 pt layout is used for essays, talk notes and the newsletter archive.
